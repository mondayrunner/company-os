company-os · explained in pictures

Your company has a memory. This is how it works.

Nine pictures, few words. For someone who has never seen it.

1 / 9

Everything lives in folders of text.

Client files, the wiki, your compass, the price list. Plain files you can open, read and change. No database you have to trust.

markdown = the truth

2 / 9

A card index makes it fast.

Next to the folders sits an index: 13,000 cards that say where things are. Throw it away and company-os index rebuilds it from the folders in a minute.

the index is cache, not source

3 / 9

Every day, notes come in.

Mail, calendar, tasks, payments, recorded calls. Small programs ("connectors") fetch them and lay them out. Figures that live elsewhere, like your revenue, are not copied but asked fresh every time.

4 / 9

You talk to it in Claude Code.

No separate screen, no chat window. You ask where you already work. The brain answers with one card: the state, whose move it is, the last contact. Not a list of 46 files.

MCP = the plug between Claude and the brain

5 / 9

The brain does not think. It answers.

There used to be another AI inside the brain that went searching when you asked. Slow and expensive. Now the brain only returns facts, straight from the folders. The thinking is done by the AI you talk to.

6 / 9

Making is free. Sending is yours.

A task on Trello, a draft in Proton, a note in a file: the AI may do that right away, because you can undo it. There is no button for "send". That one is yours.

every action shows in Activity

7 / 9

The dashboard watches along.

The screen at localhost:4321 does nothing by itself. It shows what is going on and follows along: create a task in Claude Code and it is on the screen within three seconds.

8 / 9

An inspector walks around every week.

Nine fixed checks, no AI: does the pipeline contradict the file? Is there a price that is not on the price list? Did a job quietly stop running? What it finds goes to your inbox. You decide.

9 / 9

What is coming.

One screen that sums up the day. Advisors you call in about one client, instead of every morning about everything. Recordings that become a proposal for the file by themselves. And a button that sends a Trello card with the whole file to Cursor.
